# Portfolio reel — shot-list (portfolio §6b)

**Deliverable:** a 2–3 minute reel of the Nocturne PostListener, **filmed by the author** on a real phone with
real hands. Nothing in this reel is auto-rendered or composited — the phone screen you see is the running app,
and the gestures are a person actually playing the instrument. This is the moving-image proof that ships with the
Media Lab (Opera of the Future) application, beside the one-screen `/statement` page (§6a).

> **What to enable before filming:** set `VITE_ENABLE_NOCTURNE=true` in the environment, and — for the fullest
> Act II — optionally the two Act-II flags `VITE_ENABLE_THRONE_INTRO_RAMP=true` (the instrument introduces itself)
> and `VITE_ENABLE_FALTER=true` (the diegetic falter). Generated per-session music is `VITE_ENABLE_LIVE_MUSIC_GEN=true`;
> leave it off and the catalog fallback is byte-identical for filming.

---

## 1. Intent — what the reel must prove

In under three minutes the reel must land four claims, in Machover's own terms. **(1) Expressive agency — "I did
that."** A natural human gesture (a lean, a strike, a turn) visibly and audibly *causes* a musical change with no
perceptible lag — this is a hyperinstrument, an amplifier of intention, not a controller. **(2) Beauty.** The thing
is poetic and sculptural — lamplight and reverb, one line of type at a time, no chrome, no meters, no numbers — it
reads as an opera staged for one person, not a dark-mode app. **(3) The unbroken seam.** Act I (writing your taste in
light) flows into Act II (conducting the music that taste summoned) through a single continuous moment where the
light widens and the reverb widens *together* — one instrument, two movements, no cut, no load screen. **(4) A
returnable practice.** The last beat shows the work is not one session but an accumulating record — an opera whose
season is a life. If a reviewer watches once and feels *a person expressively played an instrument that was
beautiful, whole, and worth returning to*, the reel has done its job.

---

## 2. Kit & capture notes (practical)

- **Two devices.** The phone runs the piece; a second camera (a second phone on a small tripod, or a friend) films
  the screen, the hands, and the face. Do not screen-record — the reel's whole argument is *real hands moving real
  light*, so we must see the phone as a physical object being played.
- **Headphones on the player, line-out for the mix.** The player wears headphones (the piece is binaural); capture
  the actual program audio clean by recording the phone's output separately (BT to the edit, or a second pass), so
  the Prompter's voice and the music sit under the picture at full fidelity. Room mic only for the felt "chk" of
  the hands, mixed low.
- **Light the hands warm, keep the room dark.** Match the stage: a warm key on the hands, near-black surround, so
  the phone screen is the brightest thing in frame and the amber light reads.
- **One continuous take per act if you can.** The seam (Shot 8) especially must be an unbroken hold — never cut
  across the bloom.
- **Landscape, shallow, steady.** 24–30fps, slight slow-motion license on the strike and the seam only.

## 4. Cutaways to capture (b-roll — shoot these separately, cut them in tight)

Three inserts carry the whole argument if a reviewer only half-watches. Get each in isolation, close, and clean:

- **The Trace being written.** Extreme close-up of the phone screen during a leanLift/listen/rise seal: a single
  stroke of light appears and **stays**. This is the visual thesis — *taste, written by the hand, in light.* Cut it
  under Shots 4–7.
- **The bloom seam.** A second, isolated pass of Shot 8 held even longer, shot slightly slow-motion, so the
  light-widening and the reverb-widening are unmistakably simultaneous. This is the money frame — over-cover it.
- **The downbeat strike.** The hand's sharp downward move and the **strike ring** on screen, in one frame, ideally
  in slow-motion so the < 100ms answer is legible to the eye. Cut it under Shot 9 (and echo it back to Shot 6 to
  show the Act-I lesson paying off in Act II).

---

## 5. Audio / voice note

**No voiceover. None.** The reel is carried entirely by the piece's own diegetic sound — the **Prompter's voice**
(her per-beat transfer lines are already the narration the reel needs: *"the orchestra will remember this lean," …
"now — the same room, opened. lift your hand."*) and **the music itself**. Capture the program audio clean and let
it play under the picture. The only added sound is the faint, low room-mic "chk" of the hands and the phone, mixed
well under, so the gestures feel physical. No score, no explainer track, no title-card music. If a moment needs
explaining in words, the staging is wrong — re-shoot the staging, don't add narration.

---

## 6. Captions & honesty (felt-personalization honesty extends to the reel)

On-screen text is minimal — a couple of quiet lower-third captions in the mono register, and one honesty caption
that is **non-negotiable**.

- **Everything you see on screen is real and live** — the gestures, the taste being written, the music (generated
  per-session when the live-music flag is on, otherwise a matched catalog track), the unbroken seam, the conducting,
  and the record of past sessions. No screen state is faked or re-created for the camera. A single caption early
  (under Shot 2 or 3) may state this plainly: *"Filmed on a live device — nothing on screen is rendered."*
- **The one exception — the collective constellation is mock data.** In Shot 11 the player's **own** past sessions
  are real (read from the on-device archive); the surrounding field of *other people's* taste-lights is placeholder
  data, because the real collective backend is deferred. **This must be captioned on screen** the moment the field
  appears: *"The wider field is placeholder data — the collective backend is not yet built."* The app itself already
  says as much in one honest line; the reel repeats it. Do not imply a live network of listeners we do not have.

Keep captions sparse, low-opacity, mono, and gone quickly — they are footnotes to the light, never a UI on top of it.

---

## 7. Pacing summary

Overture + name-slip (0:18) → Act I four beats + face (0:57) → **the seam (0:14, held, uncut)** → Throne (0:34) →
end + Coda + constellation (0:33) ≈ **2:36**. Front-load nothing; give the seam and the Throne room to breathe;
end quiet. If you must cut for length, trim Act I beats 5–7 before touching Shots 4, 8, or 9 — the first *I-did-that*,
the seam, and the sustained conducting are the reel's spine.
